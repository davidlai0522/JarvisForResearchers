## TL;DR
Posterior Matching Distillation (PMD) reframes diffusion distillation by supervising a stochastic student model to recover the teacher's full conditional posterior $q(x | z_t)$ using exponentially tilted means. This is made tractable via the Tilt=Shift Identity, allowing the objective to be minimized using a single evaluation of the teacher at a shifted input, thereby enabling superior sample diversity and efficient, any-step distillation.

## The Problem
Diffusion distillation seeks to compress the iterative sampling process of a large, pretrained teacher model into a few forward passes of a smaller student model. Existing distribution-based distillation methods, such as Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD), rely on reverse-KL objectives. A critical limitation of these reverse-KL approaches is their inherent mode-seeking bias. This bias causes the student to disproportionately favor the dominant modes present in the teacher's distribution, leading to a degradation in sample diversity at the expense of fidelity to less frequent outcomes. Furthermore, prior work has shown that simply matching generated distributions does not guarantee the preservation of the underlying relationship between noisy inputs and clean samples, and Moment Matching (MM) often compares conditional means at an artificially constructed noisy state rather than the original input state.

## Key Contributions
PMD introduces three primary advancements:
1. **Direct posterior supervision:** We formulate a conditional distribution-matching objective based on exponentially tilted means, a formulation made computationally accessible through the Tilt=Shift Identity.
2. **Posterior identification and any-step sampling:** We prove that minimizing the zero population loss uniquely identifies the full conditional posterior, which implies that an exact posterior student can reproduce the target distribution without requiring retraining for different step counts.
3. **Superior Conditional Diversity:** We demonstrate empirically that PMD consistently surpasses competing methods in intra-prompt diversity across all evaluated metrics while simultaneously achieving the best FID among four-step distillation methods.

## How It Works

PMD trains a stochastic student $G_\theta$ to learn the teacher's conditional distribution $q(x | z_t)$ at every noise level $t$. The core mechanism involves matching the exponentially reweighted averages of samples generated by the student, conditioned on a fixed noisy state $z_t$, against the corresponding target posterior means derived from the teacher. The Tilt=Shift Identity is central to this process; it establishes that for Gaussian corruption, the mean of a tilted posterior $q_\lambda(x | z_t) = e^{\lambda^\top x} q(x | z_t)$ is equivalent to a single evaluation of the teacher model $D_t$ at a shifted input, $D_t(z_t + \rho_t\lambda)$. PMD minimizes the expected squared posterior matching residual, $L_{PMD}(\theta)$, across noise levels, noisy inputs, and tilt vectors $\lambda$, effectively supervising the entire conditional distribution via a family of mean constraints.

### Stochastic Student $G_\theta$
The student model, $G_\theta$, is designed as a stochastic denoiser. It generates a sample $\hat{x} = G_\theta(z_t, u, t)$, where $u \sim N(0, I)$ is an independent random input. The stochasticity introduced by $u$ allows $G_\theta$ to define a conditional distribution $p_{G_\theta}(x | z_t)$, which is the target for matching against the teacher's posterior.

### Frozen Teacher Denoiser $D_t$
The teacher model, $D_t$, remains fixed and pretrained. Its role is to compute the target quantity: the mean of the exponentially tilted posterior, $D_t(z_t + \rho_t\lambda)$. This is achieved by performing a single forward pass of the teacher at the input state $z_t$ perturbed by the scaled tilt vector $\rho_t\lambda$.

### Tilt Vector $\lambda$
The vector $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}^d$ serves as the parameter defining the exponential tilt. By varying $\lambda$, we define the tilted posterior $q_\lambda(x | z_t) = e^{\lambda^\top x} q(x | z_t)$. This mechanism allows the objective to probe and enforce constraints on different modes of the posterior distribution, moving beyond simple mean matching.

### Posterior Matching Loss $L_{PMD}(\theta)$
The objective function is defined as the expected squared posterior matching residual:
$$L_{PMD}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t, z_t, \lambda} \left[ \left\| \frac{\mathbb{E}_u[\hat{x}e^{\lambda^\top\hat{x}}]}{\mathbb{E}_u[e^{\lambda^\top\hat{x}}]} - D_t(z_t + \rho_t\lambda) \right\|^2 \right]$$
This loss forces the expected value of the student's tilted samples to match the teacher's known tilted posterior mean.

### Auxiliary GAN Loss $\gamma_{GAN}L_{GAN}$
To further enhance fidelity and mode coverage, we augment the primary PMD objective with an auxiliary non-saturating logistic GAN loss, $\gamma_{GAN}L_{GAN}$. This loss utilizes a trainable discriminator head that operates on caption-conditioned features extracted from the frozen teacher model, providing an additional supervisory signal beyond the distribution matching.

## Why This Matters
PMD provides a significant methodological advance in diffusion model compression. By leveraging the Tilt=Shift Identity, it circumvents the need for computationally expensive Monte Carlo sampling from the teacher's posterior, replacing it with a single, efficient evaluation at a shifted input. Furthermore, because the method learns the conditional posterior at every noise level $t$, it inherently supports any-step sampling budgets without requiring the training of specialized student models for different step counts. The empirical results confirm that this approach yields superior sample quality and diversity compared to existing distillation techniques.

## Limitations & Open Questions
The theoretical foundation of PMD rests on the assumption that the teacher denoiser $D_t$ is perfectly exact. Additionally, the optimization process requires that the student's exponential moments remain finite within an open neighborhood surrounding $\lambda=0$. Future work should investigate the robustness of the posterior identification proof under inexact teacher models and explore methods to constrain the required moment finiteness during training.
