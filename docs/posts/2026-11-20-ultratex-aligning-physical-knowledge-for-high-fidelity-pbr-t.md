## TL;DR
UltraTex is a cascaded multi-view diffusion framework that generates high-fidelity Physically Based Rendering (PBR) texture maps by explicitly integrating physical knowledge. It achieves this by using a Multi-Modal Transformer and Multi-View Transformer for initial geometry/albedo prediction, followed by a diffusion stage for metallic-roughness (MR) map generation, which is conditioned by a VLM-guided intrinsic material adapter and guided by photometric representation alignment.

## The Problem
Generating accurate PBR texture maps, such as metallic and roughness, from a single reference image is inherently ill-posed. The observed image appearance is a complex entanglement of intrinsic material properties (like albedo, metallic, roughness), surface geometry, and the specific illumination conditions under which the image was captured. Current generative approaches often fail because they rely solely on strong appearance priors, which are insufficient to disentangle these factors, particularly when the goal is to enable accurate relighting.

The gaps in prior work are threefold: existing generative methods struggle to infer the necessary metallic and roughness attributes for robust relighting; relying only on appearance priors neglects the need for explicit material understanding; and many prior PBR methods fail to incorporate generative priors end-to-end across large-scale PBR datasets, limiting their generalization capacity.

## Key Contributions
We present the first diffusion-based PBR texture generation framework that explicitly aligns physical knowledge through material-semantic conditioning and photometric representation guidance. We introduce a VLM-guided intrinsic material adapter that translates hidden-layer vision-language features into material-aware tokens, effectively injecting part-wise semantic priors into the MR generation process. Furthermore, we propose photometric representation alignment, a mechanism that guides intermediate MR diffusion features using controlled-light material renderings to accurately capture fine-grained material-light interactions.

UltraTex operates as a cascaded two-stage multi-view diffusion pipeline. Stage 1 focuses on establishing global geometric and albedo consistency. It jointly predicts orthographic-view albedo maps and world-space shading normals using a Multi-Modal Transformer (MM-T) and a Multi-View Transformer (MV-T). Stage 2 then predicts the metallic-roughness (MR) maps. This prediction is conditioned on the albedo generated in Stage 1, features extracted from the reference image, and injected material semantic priors. These priors are realized via the VLM-guided intrinsic material adapter, which converts frozen VLM hidden states into 1D intrinsic material tokens. Crucially, photometric representation alignment guides the intermediate MR diffusion features toward ground-truth material renderings under controlled lighting, utilizing a frozen DINOv2 encoder to bridge the domain gap between observed and physically simulated renderings.

### Multi-Modal Transformer (MM-T)
The MM-T is responsible for initial feature fusion within individual views. It fuses various conditioning tokens—which can include image tokens ($T_{img}$), geometric tokens ($T_{geo}$), or combined albedo and material tokens ($T_{img}, T_{alb}, T_{mat}$)—alongside the noisy view latents specific to that view. This fusion is performed using $l1$ transformer blocks equipped with intra-view self-attention mechanisms.

### Multi-View Transformer (MV-T)
To ensure global consistency across the predicted maps, the MV-T models cross-view interactions. It aggregates the view-wise latents ($\hat{z}_i$) into a unified sequence. This sequence is then processed through $l2$ Transformer blocks, enabling the model to enforce global coherence across the multi-view predictions.

### VLM-guided intrinsic material adapter (A)
This component serves as the bridge between high-level semantic understanding and low-level generative tasks. It takes concatenated intermediate visual tokens ($H_{vlm}^M$) extracted from a frozen Vision-Language Model (VLM). The adapter projects these tokens into a sequence of 1D intrinsic material tokens ($T_{mat}$), thereby injecting explicit, part-wise semantic priors into the subsequent MR generation stage.

### Photometric representation alignment
This mechanism enforces physical plausibility during the MR diffusion process. It employs a frozen DINOv2 encoder ($f$) to extract target representations ($y$) from renderings ($x_{pm}$) generated under controlled lighting conditions. These target representations then guide the intermediate MR diffusion features via the $L_{REPA}$ loss, ensuring that the generated MR maps correspond accurately to how materials should interact with light.

## Why This Matters
For practitioners in computer graphics and robotics, this work demonstrates that appearance matching alone is insufficient for high-fidelity PBR generation; the explicit injection of physical knowledge is necessary for robust synthesis. The cascaded diffusion pipeline structure allows for a logical, sequential refinement process: first, establishing the fundamental geometry and albedo, and subsequently refining the complex intrinsic material properties like metallic and roughness. Moreover, the integration of VLM features via specialized adapters provides a viable, scalable mechanism to inject rich, high-level semantic priors directly into low-level, pixel-wise generative tasks.

## Limitations & Open Questions
The primary limitation of the current formulation is its reliance on a cascaded two-stage pipeline, which inherently introduces sequential dependencies where errors in Stage 1 propagate to Stage 2. Additionally, the photometric representation alignment component necessitates the availability of ground-truth materials rendered under a controlled point-light setup during the training phase, which can be a practical constraint. Future work should investigate end-to-end training strategies to mitigate sequential dependency issues.
